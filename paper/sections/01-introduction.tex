\section{Introduction}

\label{sec:introduction}

Consensus protocols coordinate replicas so that a replicated state machine
can apply operations consistently despite concurrent execution and replica
failures. Two widely studied design approaches differ in how they establish
this coordination. Leader-based protocols such as Raft~\cite{ongaro2014raft}
direct client operations through an elected leader, whereas leaderless
protocols such as EPaxos~\cite{moraru2013epaxos} allow replicas to propose
operations independently and determine the dependencies required for
consistent execution.

These architectural differences motivate empirical comparison, but a direct
protocol-level ranking is difficult to interpret. Measured throughput and
latency depend on replica count, client concurrency, workload composition,
conflict rate, and communication conditions. They are also affected by
implementation mechanisms including persistence, request processing,
scheduling, transport, and message handling. The presence or absence of a
leader is therefore an important structural property, but it does not by
itself determine the performance or failure behaviour of a concrete
implementation, so a measured difference between two implementations cannot
automatically be attributed to the consensus algorithm alone.

In this paper, we experimentally compare Raft and EPaxos as representative
leader-based and leaderless consensus protocols, measuring throughput,
latency, resource utilisation, and failure behaviour under controlled
variation of system and workload conditions.

We investigate the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{RQ1:} How do throughput and latency change with workload and
system conditions, including read/write ratio, client concurrency, conflict
rate, and replica count?

\item \textbf{RQ2:} How do the evaluated Raft and EPaxos implementations
differ in failure behaviour and in their sensitivity to communication cost?

\item \textbf{RQ3:} To what extent are the observed performance differences
between Raft and EPaxos attributable to implementation choices rather than
to the protocol families themselves?

\end{itemize}

We contribute a reproducible experimental framework that executes both
protocol families in a common containerised environment using a shared
client harness and workload generator. The complete benchmark and analysis
artefacts are released at
\url{https://github.com/Sephy314/Leader-consensus-comparison}.

We further contribute a sensitivity-oriented evaluation rather than a
single-configuration benchmark, varying replica count, client concurrency,
read/write composition, conflict rate, failure mode, and communication
conditions.

Finally, we provide an implementation-level analysis using multiple
implementations of the two protocol families and matched persistence
configurations, showing that implementation mechanisms can materially
change the measured performance relationship and, under matched storage
conditions, reverse the ordering observed in the baseline configuration.

The experiments are conducted in a local containerised environment, so the
reported values are measurements of the evaluated implementations under the
specified experimental conditions rather than universal estimates of
production performance.

The remainder of the paper reviews related work (Section~\ref{sec:background}),
describes the experimental methodology (Section~\ref{sec:setup}), presents and
interprets the results (Sections~\ref{sec:evaluation} and~\ref{sec:discussion}),
and discusses threats to validity and conclusions (Sections~\ref{sec:threats}
and~\ref{sec:conclusion}).
