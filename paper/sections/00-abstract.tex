\begin{abstract}

Leader-based consensus protocols such as Raft route normal write requests
through an elected leader, whereas leaderless protocols such as EPaxos permit
proposal initiation at multiple replicas. This paper presents an
implementation-level experimental comparison of these two approaches under a
common benchmark methodology. The evaluation uses the same workload generator,
CPU and memory allocations, failure injection, and measurement procedures,
with ten independent repetitions across seven experiment families and replica
counts from three to nine on a single containerised host.

In the primary implementations, EPaxos achieved higher mean throughput than
Raft at every tested replica count, with mean ratios ranging from
1.29$\times$ to 1.75$\times$. The ratio also varied across experiment
families that exercise the same nominal configuration: the four steady-state
families measured EPaxos/Raft ratios of 1.15, 1.25, 1.22, and 1.51, so the
headline 1.51$\times$ comes from the family in which Raft measured lowest.
The relative difference varied with cluster size, and confidence intervals
overlapped at seven and nine replicas. Increasing the replica count reduced
throughput for both implementations, showing that larger replica sets
introduced additional coordination and communication costs rather than
proportional throughput scaling. Client concurrency produced a larger
separation at high concurrency, while the read/write-ratio and
conflict-ratio experiments showed no clear systematic throughput trend.
Median latency also depended on operating condition, with the ordering
between implementations changing across the concurrency range. Resource
measurements showed similar aggregate CPU utilisation but different
distributions across replicas, while EPaxos used approximately 1.8$\times$ the
peak memory of Raft in the evaluated implementations.

Failure experiments revealed a more specific distinction in request-path
dependence. Losing the Raft leader produced availability gaps of
1.81--4.74\,s and thousands of failed requests, whereas losing a Raft
follower or an EPaxos replica produced substantially shorter interruptions in
the tested three-replica configuration. These results characterise specific
failure modes rather than establishing a general fault-tolerance advantage for
either protocol.

To examine implementation sensitivity, the study additionally evaluates two
Raft implementations and two EPaxos implementations. Their responses to
application-level one-way communication delay differed substantially: at
10\,ms of injected cost, throughput decreased by 72--96\,\% across the
implementations, while the etcd Raft implementation decreased by 94\,\% at
1\,ms. The delay also reversed the baseline ordering: at 5\,ms and above,
HashiCorp Raft sustained higher throughput than either EPaxos implementation
(2{,}316 vs 1{,}432 and 479\,req/s at 5\,ms), so the leader-based/leaderless
ranking is not invariant to communication conditions. These results show
that absolute performance and communication sensitivity depend substantially
on the implementation stack and cannot be attributed to consensus structure
alone. A separate correctness harness performed 18 deterministic
state-convergence tests covering concurrency levels of 1, 8, and 32 and the
tested replica-failure scenarios; all tests passed. The results should
therefore be interpreted as controlled measurements of the evaluated
implementations rather than universal properties of Raft or EPaxos.

\end{abstract}
