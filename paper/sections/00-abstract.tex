\begin{abstract}

Leader-based consensus protocols such as Raft route write requests through
an elected leader, whereas leaderless protocols such as EPaxos permit
proposal initiation at multiple replicas. This paper presents an
implementation-level comparison of these approaches under a common
benchmark methodology: the same workload generator, allocations, failure
injection, and metrics, with ten repetitions across seven experiment
families and replica counts from three to nine on a single containerised
host.

In the primary implementations, EPaxos achieved higher median throughput
than Raft at three and five replicas (ratios 1.22 and 1.73, exact
Mann-Whitney $p<0.001$), but no significant difference was detected at
seven or nine replicas. The EPaxos decline at those sizes is associated
with request timeouts at the 2{,}000\,ms client timeout, while successful
requests completed in 5.4--5.6\,ms, the same as at three replicas. Median
latency was flat for EPaxos across replica counts while Raft grew from 6.9
to 14.6\,ms, and at concurrency 256 EPaxos measured 41{,}077\,req/s against
19{,}333\,req/s for Raft. Losing the Raft leader produced availability gaps
of 1.81--4.74\,s, whereas losing a Raft follower or an EPaxos replica
produced gaps below 0.1\,s.

Evaluating two Raft and two EPaxos implementations shows that implementation
choice materially affects performance: under 10\,ms of injected one-way
delay, throughput fell by 72--96\,\% across implementations, and the etcd
Raft implementation collapsed by 94\,\% at 1\,ms, reversing the baseline
ordering. A correctness harness passed 18 deterministic state-convergence
tests across all implementations. The results are controlled measurements
of the evaluated implementations, not universal properties of Raft or
EPaxos.

\end{abstract}
