\begin{abstract}

Leader-based consensus protocols such as Raft route normal write requests
through an elected leader, whereas leaderless protocols such as EPaxos permit
proposal initiation at multiple replicas. This paper presents an
implementation-level experimental comparison of these two approaches under a
common benchmark methodology: the same workload generator, CPU and memory
allocations, failure injection, and measurement procedures, with ten
independent repetitions across seven experiment families and replica counts
from three to nine on a single containerised host.

In the primary implementations, EPaxos achieved higher median throughput than
Raft at three, five, and seven replicas (ratios 1.22, 1.73, and 1.18), but
the difference disappeared at nine replicas (ratio 0.99). Several repetitions
of both protocols stalled---Raft when the leader's snapshot work delayed its
heartbeats and triggered repeated elections, EPaxos under persistent request
timeouts---so we report medians, which are robust to these stalls, rather
than means. Median latency was flat for EPaxos (5.4--5.6\,ms) across replica
counts while Raft grew from 6.9 to 14.6\,ms. Client concurrency produced a
larger separation at high concurrency: at concurrency 256, EPaxos measured
41{,}077\,req/s against 19{,}333\,req/s for Raft, with p50 latency 5.6\,ms
against 12.1\,ms. The read/write-ratio and conflict-ratio experiments showed
no clear systematic throughput trend, and EPaxos used approximately
1.8$\times$ the peak memory of Raft.

Failure experiments revealed a more specific distinction in request-path
dependence. Losing the Raft leader produced availability gaps of
1.81--4.74\,s and thousands of failed requests, whereas losing a Raft
follower or an EPaxos replica produced substantially shorter interruptions in
the tested three-replica configuration.

To examine implementation sensitivity, the study additionally evaluates two
Raft implementations and two EPaxos implementations. Their responses to
application-level one-way communication delay differed substantially: at
10\,ms of injected cost, throughput decreased by 72--96\,\% across the
implementations, while the etcd Raft implementation decreased by 94\,\% at
1\,ms. The delay also changed the ordering: from 1\,ms onward, HashiCorp
Raft sustained higher throughput than the original EPaxos implementation
(and, at 5--10\,ms, than nvb EPaxos as well), so the leader-based/leaderless
ranking is not invariant to communication conditions. A separate correctness
harness performed 18 deterministic state-convergence tests covering
concurrency levels of 1, 8, and 32 and the tested replica-failure scenarios;
all tests passed. The results should therefore be interpreted as controlled
measurements of the evaluated implementations rather than universal
properties of Raft or EPaxos.

\end{abstract}
