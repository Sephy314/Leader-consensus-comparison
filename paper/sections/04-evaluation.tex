\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

All reported values are means over three repetitions. Unless stated
otherwise, results are for 3 replicas, 100\,\% writes, and concurrency 32.

\subsection{Throughput and Scalability}

At the 3-replica baseline (concurrency 32, 100\%\ writes), EPaxos sustains
5{,}697\,req/s and Raft 4{,}449\,req/s. One Raft repetition at this
configuration recorded 542\,req/s, an order of magnitude below the other two,
and is excluded as an outlier. Increasing the replica count reduces
throughput for both protocols, but at different rates
(Table~\ref{tab:scaling}). EPaxos falls from 5{,}310\,req/s at 5 replicas to
2{,}827\,req/s at 9 replicas, a reduction of 50\% relative to the 3-replica
baseline. Raft falls from 3{,}195\,req/s at 5 replicas to 2{,}059\,req/s at 9
replicas, a reduction of 54\%. The gap between the protocols narrows as
replicas are added: EPaxos leads Raft by 66\% at 5 replicas, 81\% at 7
replicas, and 37\% at 9 replicas.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Throughput (req/s, mean of 3 repetitions) by replica count, at
100\,\% writes and concurrency 32.}
\label{tab:scaling}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Replicas & Raft & EPaxos & EPaxos/Raft \\
\midrule
3 & 4{,}449 & 5{,}697 & 1.28$\times$ \\
5 & 3{,}195 & 5{,}310 & 1.66$\times$ \\

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

Throughput rises with client concurrency for both protocols, but the
protocols diverge at high concurrency. At concurrency 1 the request rate is
latency-bound: Raft reaches 307\,req/s and EPaxos 186\,req/s. Both scale
roughly linearly up to concurrency 64, after which Raft flattens:
at concurrency 256, EPaxos reaches 34{,}936\,req/s and Raft
17{,}039\,req/s. Across the concurrency sweep the highest per-run value
observed for EPaxos is 37{,}880\,req/s and for Raft 18{,}233\,req/s.

The write-ratio sweep shows little variation in throughput for either
protocol. Between 0\,\% and 100\,\% writes, EPaxos ranges from 4{,}585 to
4{,}621\,req/s and Raft from 4{,}488 to 4{,}674\,req/s at concurrency 32.
Reads are routed through consensus in both protocols, and neither shows a
systematic trend across the write ratios tested. The absolute values in
this family are lower than the concurrency family at the same nominal
configuration (for example, EPaxos at 100\%\ writes and concurrency 32
measures 4{,}610\,req/s here versus 5{,}697\,req/s in the concurrency
family); the families were run as separate sweeps, and the difference is
within the run-to-run variation observed across families.

Conflict ratio also produces little systematic change. Mean throughput
ranges from 3{,}897 to 4{,}448\,req/s for Raft and from 4{,}608 to
5{,}672\,req/s for EPaxos across conflict ratios of 0--100\%. The
within-configuration variation across repetitions is of comparable
magnitude to the between-configuration variation in both cases.

\subsection{Latency}

At the 3-replica baseline, median latency is 6.7\,ms for Raft and 5.6\,ms
for EPaxos (one Raft repetition recorded 57.8\,ms and is excluded as an
outlier). As replicas are added, Raft median latency grows while EPaxos
median latency is largely unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:latency}). Raft rises
from 9.7\,ms at 5 replicas to 15.1\,ms at 9 replicas; EPaxos remains between
5.4\,ms and 5.5\,ms across the same range.

Latency also differs in its response to concurrency. Both protocols show
increasing latency as concurrency rises, but EPaxos maintains a lower
median across the sweep. In the concurrency family the EPaxos median stays
near 5.6\,ms at concurrency 32 and rises at higher loads, while the Raft
median is consistently higher at the same concurrency levels.

\subsection{Resource Utilisation}

Across all runs, mean per-replica CPU utilisation is 33.3\,\% for EPaxos and
30.8\,\% for Raft, and peak per-replica RSS is 45.8\,MB for EPaxos and
26.7\,MB for Raft. Mean per-replica network rates are 340\,kB/s RX and
326\,kB/s TX for EPaxos, and 419\,kB/s RX and 412\,kB/s TX for Raft.

The distribution across replicas differs between the protocols. In the
9-replica scaling runs, Raft CPU is concentrated: the leader replica uses
about 26\,\% CPU while each follower uses about 13\,\%, a ratio of
approximately 2:1. Which replica holds the leader role varies between
repetitions, so the concentration appears on different replica indices in
different runs. In the corresponding EPaxos runs, CPU is distributed
evenly: every replica uses between 20.3\,\% and 21.8\,\%, with no replica
standing out.

\subsection{Failure Recovery Behaviour}

Failure outcomes differ by failure type (Table~\ref{tab:failure}). Killing
the Raft leader produces a write-availability gap of 1.72--2.20\,s and
1{,}003--1{,}120 failed requests, as the client waits for a new leader to be
elected. Killing a Raft follower produces a gap of 0.04--0.06\,s with no
failed requests. Killing an EPaxos replica produces a gap of
0.06--0.09\,s with 10--22 failed requests. All runs complete with status
\texttt{success}; the failed requests are those issued during the transition.

failure injection.}

The election family, which isolates replicas to force repeated failed
elections during a Raft leader failure, produces gaps from 0.02\,s to
12.14\,s depending on how many elections fail. Gaps increase with the number
of failed elections for a given repetition, and the spread across
repetitions is wider than in the plain leader-kill case.

\subsection{Summary of Findings}

The measurements support the following observations under the conditions
tested:

\begin{itemize}
\item EPaxos sustains higher throughput than Raft at every replica count
  tested, with the largest relative difference at 7 replicas
  (1.81$\times$).
\item Throughput decreases with replica count for both protocols; EPaxos
  decreases by 50\% and Raft by 54\% from 3 to 9 replicas.
\item EPaxos reaches a higher peak throughput at high client concurrency
  (34{,}936\,req/s versus 17{,}039\,req/s at concurrency 256).
\item Median latency is lower for EPaxos at every replica count, and grows
  with replica count only for Raft.
\item Per-replica CPU is concentrated on the leader in Raft (about 2:1
  leader-to-follower) and evenly distributed in EPaxos.
\item Write ratio and conflict ratio produce no systematic throughput trend
  in either protocol over the ranges tested.
\item Leader failure in Raft costs 1.72--2.20\,s of write availability and
  about 1{,}000 failed requests, while follower failure and EPaxos replica
  failure cost under 0.1\,s with at most 22 failed requests.
\end{itemize}
