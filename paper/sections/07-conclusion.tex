\section{Conclusion}

\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper compared Raft and EPaxos as representative leader-based and leaderless designs, examining throughput, latency, replica scaling, client concurrency, failure recovery, resource use, and communication sensitivity under controlled variation rather than a single benchmark configuration.

The workload and scaling experiments show that the measured relationship depends on the operating condition: replica count reduced throughput for both implementations, while high client concurrency favoured the evaluated EPaxos configuration, whose proposal path is distributed across replicas rather than centred on an elected leader. The measurements do not establish that this structural difference is the sole cause. Neither implementation responded in a simple or monotonic way to workload composition, and among the four implementations only nvb EPaxos responded to the configured conflict fraction.

The failure experiments identify a distinct consequence of the leader-centred request path: leader failure interrupted writes until a replacement was elected, whereas follower and EPaxos replica failure produced interruptions two orders of magnitude shorter in the tested three-replica configuration, and recovery related to the number of disrupted elections. These measurements describe the tested recovery configuration rather than protocol-independent bounds.

The implementation-sensitivity experiments provide the strongest qualification to direct protocol comparisons. Implementations of the same family differed in absolute performance and in their response to injected communication cost, and persistence, request handling, transport, scheduling, and adapter behaviour can change measured throughput and latency without changing the algorithm. Absolute results are therefore properties of the evaluated implementation and environment rather than intrinsic constants of Raft or EPaxos.

Taken together, the results do not establish a universal ordering between leader-based and leaderless consensus: the relationship depends jointly on consensus structure, implementation, workload, replica count, concurrency, communication conditions, and failure mode. The evaluated Raft configuration exposes the concentration and failure dependency of a leader-centred path, while the evaluated EPaxos configuration exposes the trade-off of distributed proposal placement and dependency coordination.

The study is limited by its single-host containerised environment, operating-system-level emulation rather than a physical network, the tested cluster sizes, the closed-loop workload, and the specific implementations evaluated; the correctness experiments establish convergence under the tested workloads and failures rather than linearizability. These limitations restrict generalisation to production or geographically distributed deployments.

The methodology nevertheless provides a reproducible basis for studying consensus implementations under controlled change in workload and system conditions. Extending it to further implementations, larger and multi-host clusters, profiling, and workloads that exercise stronger contention would help separate protocol-level effects from implementation behaviour.
